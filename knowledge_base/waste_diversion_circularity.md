# Waste Diversion, Contamination Control & Circular Economy (SDG 12 Alignment)

## 1. Campus Waste Characterization & Baselines
- **Baseline Generation:** A typical higher education / corporate campus produces 0.35 to 0.65 kg of total solid waste per full-time occupant per day.
- **Composition Breakdown:**
  - Organic / Compostable Food Waste: 35% - 45% (concentrated in dining facilities, Building C)
  - Recyclable Paper, Cardboard & Fiber: 25% - 30% (concentrated in academic/admin, Buildings A & D)
  - Plastics, Metals & Glass: 10% - 15%
  - Non-divertible Residual Landfill: 15% - 25%

## 2. Waste Diversion Rate Standards
- **Zero Waste International Alliance (ZWIA) Standard:** Minimum 90% overall diversion from landfill and incineration.
- **Campus Target Milestones:**
  - Bronze Tier: > 45% diversion rate
  - Silver Tier: > 60% diversion rate
  - Gold / High Performance: > 75% diversion rate

## 3. Detecting Waste Anomalies & Contamination
- **Symptom: Sudden Drop in Diversion Rate (< 35%):**
  - *Cause:* Single-stream recycling contamination rate exceeded hauling contractor threshold (>15%), causing entire dumpsters to be rejected and sent to landfill.
  - *Cause:* Kitchen staff turnover in dining hall resulting in pre-consumer compost being mixed into standard trash bins.
- **Symptom: Heavy Spikes in Landfill Weight:**
  - *Cause:* End-of-semester move-out or facility renovation construction debris disposed into municipal trash compactors instead of dedicated salvage bins.

## 4. Best Practice Operational Actions
- **Standardized Bin Pairing:** Every landfill receptacle must be co-located with dedicated recycling and organics bins with clear color-coded pictorial signage.
- **Back-of-House Dining Separation:** Implement dedicated food prep scrap sorting stations with daily weigh-ins to incentivize kitchen culinary waste reduction.
- **Contamination Audits:** Conduct monthly random sampling audits at centralized loading docks to identify specific building sources of non-conforming materials.
