# Campus Water Conservation Protocols & Leak Diagnostics (SDG 6 Alignment)

## 1. Minimum Night Flow (MNF) Analysis
- **Definition & Benchmark:** Between 02:00 AM and 04:30 AM, academic and administrative building water demand is virtually zero (< 0.15 m³/hour or ~150 L/hour for baseline fixture pressurization and safety equipment).
- **Leak Indicator Threshold:** If continuous night flow consistently exceeds 0.50 m³/hour (500 L/hour) during unoccupied hours for more than 2 consecutive hours, there is an 85% probability of an uncontained plumbing leak, running fixture, or stuck cooling tower blowdown valve.

## 2. Typical Sources of Water Anomalies
- **Continuous Low-to-Moderate Flow (0.2 – 1.0 m³/h overnight):**
  - Stuck flapper valves or auto-flush sensor loops in commercial restrooms.
  - Urinal flushometer solenoid failure stuck open.
  - Reverse Osmosis (RO) water purification backwash loop stuck in drain cycle.
- **Sudden Step-Change Increase (1.5 – 5.0+ m³/h continuous):**
  - Underground irrigation line rupture or sprinkler head fracture.
  - Chiller cooling tower make-up float valve failure overflowing directly into storm drains.
  - Domestic hot water boiler expansion relief valve venting continuously.
- **Dining / Food Service Center (Building C):**
  - Commercial dish machine booster rinse cycles left running between shifts.
  - Walk-in cooler water-cooled condensers running full flow without thermostatic regulation.

## 3. Recommended Remediation Protocols
- **Immediate Triage:** Dispatch facilities plumbing technician to verify sub-meter isolation valves.
- **Acoustic Leak Detection:** Deploy ultrasonic listening devices on main distribution branches if physical surface water is not visible.
- **Smart Meter Submetering:** Sub-meter cooling towers, irrigation zones, and commercial kitchens separately from main building feeds to isolate anomaly origins within 15 minutes.
